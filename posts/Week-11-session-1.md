Before diving into Figma, I started by sketching some wireframes on paper. It felt more natural to think with a pen in hand – no rules, no grids, just ideas flowing freely. Out of all the drafts I came up with, I realized that the first and second ones seemed to work best for my portfolio. Their structure felt clean and straightforward, and they included all the key parts I wanted to show: an introduction to my main project, space to explain my ideas, and a place to link to other works.

Some of the other wireframes I drew didn’t quite hit the mark. Either they didn’t show enough of the information I needed, or the layout felt a little too messy or unbalanced. But I don’t see them as failures – just stepping stones. They gave me new directions to think about, and I’m planning to keep working on them, maybe tweak a few elements or try mixing parts of different layouts together.

What surprised me the most was how much this process helped me grow creatively. I didn’t expect that simply sketching would challenge me to think in so many different ways. It made me more aware of how layout affects the way people experience a website – not just how it looks, but how it feels to navigate. Now, I find myself thinking, “What if I moved this here?” or “How can I make this section stand out more?” and it’s actually fun.

This might just be the beginning, but it’s a step that’s helped me connect more with my own ideas. I’m excited to keep experimenting and hopefully come up with something that really reflects who I am and the stories I want to tell through my work.
