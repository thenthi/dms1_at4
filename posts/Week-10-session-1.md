# Assignmanet 1 - Video Edit

**1. • How did you approach the relationship between the sonic and visual components of your edit?**

I carefully used sound to mirror the emotional arc of the visuals. The calm opening music matched the quiet, indoor shots, while upbeat music accompanied my study and social life in Australia. As I transitioned to the imagined future, I used inspirational audio, ending with gentle music as I “wake up” from the dream.

**2. • How did you decide on the footage that you used to shape your project?**

I selected clips from my everyday life—studying, walking around campus, and spending time with friends—because they honestly represent my current journey and the steps I’m taking toward my goals.

**3. • What did you learn about the power of sound to shape emotional experience, and how did you apply this knowledge to your project?**

I learned that sound isn’t just a background element—it changes the way viewers feel. Shifting the tone of the music helped me highlight different emotional states: peace, motivation, joy, and reflection. This helped me guide the viewer through the stages of my story.

**4. • How does editing relate to the concepts of pacing and rhythm, and how did you use these concepts to shape your use of time in the project?**

I used slower cuts at the beginning and end to reflect calm and introspection, while the middle section had quicker cuts and transitions to match the energy of university life. The pacing change helped emphasize growth and ambition.

**5. • Which parts of your personality, interests, past, and potential future did you choose to depict in your portrait?**

I focused on my current dedication to learning and personal growth. The video shows my curiosity, ambition, and dreams of success—especially in the imagined future scene, where I reflect who I hope to become.

**6. • What were the primary aesthetic choices that you made to express those ideas in your work?**

I used warm lighting and natural settings to make the video feel authentic and relatable. In the imagined future, I used more dramatic framing and brighter tones to represent confidence and hope. The final quiet moment returning to reality adds a soft emotional closure.

# Assignment 2 - Graphic Icons

https://thenthi-at2-icon-70.deno.dev/

**1. • How would you summarise your idea behind the suite of icons, and why did you choose the theme that you did?**

The main concept behind my icon set is to explore and preserve the essence of Vietnamese legacy through visual design. I wanted to highlight elements that represent who we are as a culture—our history, our traditions, our values. My icons represent key aspects of Vietnamese heritage: traditional outfits like the áo dài, iconic dishes, folk instruments, architectural shifts from stilt houses to modern buildings, and legendary symbols like the dragon, buffalo, and phoenix. These animals were chosen not just for their cultural significance, but also for their symbolic connection to Vietnamese identity—strength, resilience, and beauty.

**2. • Did you iterate through a series of approaches before settling on one? What changed across those iterations, and why did you choose the one that you ended up with?**

Yes, I experimented with multiple styles and compositions. My initial sketches were more illustrative and detailed, inspired by traditional drawings and folk art. However, I quickly realized that too much detail would make the icons less effective, especially when scaled down. So I shifted toward a cleaner, flatter style while keeping symbolic elements intact. The biggest change was simplifying lines and reducing visual clutter—choosing clarity over realism. The version I settled on balances tradition with modern design sensibility.

**3. • How did you translate your thumbnail sketches into the final flat designs in Illustrator? Was anything lost in that translation, or gained?**

I carefully selected the best thumbnail sketches from my initial concepts—those with the strongest visual clarity and cultural meaning—and re-created them in Illustrator. However, I encountered some challenges, especially with icons that had open or unconnected lines, such as my phoenix. The fine and fragmented linework made it difficult to apply color using standard fill tools. I had to manually close paths or rethink the shapes while still preserving the visual essence of the sketch. While some organic quality from the hand-drawn versions was lost, I gained precision, consistency, and a cleaner final look.

**4. • How did you use colour to create a sense of unity and visual interest in the icons? Why did you choose the palette that you did?**

I chose a colorful palette to convey a sense of vibrancy, energy, and pride—qualities that strongly represent Vietnamese heritage. The use of bold and dynamic colors gives the icons a heroic and powerful tone, which fits the theme of legacy and tradition. These color choices also help the icons stand out as celebratory and meaningful, not just decorative.

**5. • How did the elements and principles of sound that you used in your audio design correspond to the elements and principles of design that you used in your visual design?**

Each icon is accompanied by sounds directly related to its meaning: bird sounds for the phoenix, the sound of a house being built for architecture, the flapping of the Vietnamese flag, birdsong over rice fields, and fire crackling from a bánh chưng stove. These audio elements bring the icons to life and create an immersive experience. Just like the colors and shapes in the visuals, the sound design reinforces the mood and cultural context—whether it’s peaceful, festive, or proud. Both the audio and visual elements work together rhythmically to strengthen the narrative and emotional impact of the icons.



**6. • How did you translate the prompt that you chose from an abstract idea to a set of concrete visual designs?**

"Legacy" is a broad, abstract concept, so I began by researching what defines Vietnamese legacy to me personally and culturally. I categorized ideas into themes: tangible objects (rice grain, áo dài), structures (stilt house), and intangible symbols (mythical animals, traditional music). From there, I brainstormed visuals that could represent each category clearly and symbolically. I used sketching to test which ideas translated best into simple forms, and eventually shaped those into vector designs that maintain meaning while working as icons.

# Assignment 3 - Virtual Environment

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/VxNkByxH1wQ?si=QX2XKSY8mB5X8VL2" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

**1. • How important was a working prototype to your design process? What did it offer that process?**

The working prototype played a crucial role in helping me test whether players could truly feel the emotional journey I was trying to convey. Through early testing, I realized that some elements—such as lighting or audio cues—were not clearly communicating the intended feelings. The prototype allowed me to refine transitions, timing, and spatial flow to better guide the user through each encounter.

**2. • How did your experience of designing and producing a three-dimensional environment differ from your experience of designing and producing a project in two-dimensional media?**

Designing in 3D gave me more freedom to tell a story through space, time, and interaction. In 2D, everything must fit into a fixed frame. In contrast, the 3D environment allowed me to guide users through each emotional stage of my personal learning journey by using space and movement. The sense of immersion made the storytelling feel more powerful and personal.

**3. • What was the role of the virtual camera in your project, and how was it similar to and different from the role of the camera in Assignment 1?**

In this project, the camera allowed the player to explore freely and experience the world from a first-person perspective. This interactive viewpoint is very different from Assignment 1, where the camera was fixed or edited to follow a scripted character. The key difference is the sense of agency—here, the player moves through the story instead of just watching it unfold.

**4. • How did you use sound in your project, and how was this similar to and different from your use of sound in Assignments 1 and 2?**

Sound played a major role in shaping the emotional rhythm of the project. In Encounters 1 and 2, I used eerie, unsettling sounds like crying and whispers to express confusion and anxiety. In Encounters 3 and 4, I switched to peaceful ambient sounds to reflect hope and clarity. This approach was much more dynamic than in Assignments 1 and 2, where sound was more of a background element. Here, it helped drive the narrative.

**5. • How did you unify the different elements of your virtual environment under a certain theme or concept? How did your theme or concept evolve over the project’s development?**

The main theme—my personal journey through learning difficulties—remained consistent throughout the project. Each encounter represented a stage in that journey: confusion, anxiety, breakthrough, insight, and inner peace. As I developed the project, I refined details like color, lighting, and pacing to better express each stage, but the overall narrative direction stayed the same.

**6. • What did you discover through the process of play testing that you wouldn’t have discovered otherwise?**

Playtesting revealed that some users didn’t fully understand the emotional meaning of each encounter without additional guidance. As a result, I added clearer visual cues, directional lighting, and more expressive sound design to help communicate the mood more effectively. These adjustments helped improve the storytelling and user experience in ways I couldn’t have predicted without real feedback.
