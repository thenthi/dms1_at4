# Group Feedback
![alttext](feedback.png)
![alttext](feedback1.png)

In today's class, I shared the link to my portfolio website with my group members to gather feedback. Overall, the responses were quite positive — my peers appreciated the detailed reflections in my writing and complimented the clean, aesthetically pleasing design of the site. However, they also pointed out an important issue: there was too much information presented, some of which felt unnecessary or overwhelming for the viewer.

As mentioned in previous blog entries, I originally structured the website into four main sections:

About Me

Process Documentation (Blog)

Project Pages

Semester Timeline

Based on the feedback, I realized that while my intention was to be thorough, the Semester Timeline section added too much complexity and repetition, without contributing significantly to the user’s understanding of my work. In response, I made the decision to remove the Semester Timeline and focus on just three core sections.

I also revised and condensed the written content, trimming down some of the longer paragraphs while still preserving the key messages. These changes not only made the website more concise and user-friendly but also helped clarify the main narrative I wanted to communicate.

This process reminded me that good design isn’t just about adding — it’s about editing thoughtfully. Listening to others and being willing to simplify can lead to a stronger, clearer outcome.

After receiving feedback from my group, I decided to remove the Semester Timeline section from my website. Instead, I focused on enhancing the readability of my content by breaking long paragraphs into shorter sections with clear titles. This made it easier for viewers to understand the structure of my process and follow my journey more effectively. I also shortened some of the text while keeping the main ideas, ensuring the site remains informative but not overwhelming.
